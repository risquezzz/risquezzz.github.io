1. Executive summary

IMETAL appears in 40 of 90 answers produced by ChatGPT, Gemini and Perplexity when asked Venezuelan oilfield buying questions: 44.4% visibility.

The headline hides the useful part. The brand shows up when the buyer has already decided to request a quote, and all but disappears while they are still learning:

  • Transactional: 66.7% visibility.
  • Commercial: 56.7%.
  • Informational: 30.0%.
  • Comparative: 26.7%.

Inside the informational block there is a complete gap: across the six purely conceptual prompts — how a major overhaul is performed, what criteria to use when selecting a CNC shop, which pump types require specialised maintenance — IMETAL does not appear once in 18 observations.

Three further findings:

  1. Mention is not citation. 40 answers name the brand, but only 22 (24.4%) cite imetal.com.ve as a source. Without a citation, the mention is reconstructed from memory and may be gone in the next session.
  2. The engines disagree. ChatGPT mentions the brand in 60% of prompts, Perplexity in 40%, Gemini in 33.3%. Auditing a single engine is not a measurement, it is an anecdote.
  3. Two service lines are invisible. Drilling-rig logistics: 0 of 9. Compressor maintenance: 0 of 3.

Conclusion: IMETAL competes well at the moment of purchase and loses at the moment of criteria. In AI search that matters more than in classic SEO, because the synthesised answer replaces the first technical conversation with the client.

3. Company overview

IMETAL, C.A. — El Tigre, Anzoátegui state, Venezuela. Industrial services company serving the energy and oil sector, with more than 35 years of operation (founded 1990, per its own site).

Declared service lines:

  1. Machining centre (Work Shop): fabrication and rebuilding of parts and spares for pumps, valves and industrial equipment using CNC technology.
  2. Equipment maintenance: preventive, corrective and predictive in the field, plus major overhaul in the workshop.
  3. Rotating equipment rebuilding: centrifugal, reciprocating and screw pumps; centrifugal and reciprocating compressors; rotary heads and electric motors.
  4. Infrastructure: civil and mechanical construction, piping, tanks and production facilities.
  5. Drilling-rig logistics (stated in the company profile).

Target buyers: oil operators, joint ventures, drilling and maintenance contractors, and private operators in eastern Venezuela.

Sources: corporate site imetal.com.ve; engine responses; public records consulted during the competitive analysis.

4. Methodology

4.1 Prompt design

30 prompts written as real buying questions, in Spanish, with no brand name included. Classified by intent:

IntentPromptsWhat it measures
Informational10Discovery: how it works, what criteria to use
Commercial10Supplier search: who does it
Comparative5Evaluating alternatives
Transactional5Contact and quotation

No prompt contained "IMETAL". This is deliberate: we measure whether the brand appears without being named, which is the only visibility that brings new clients.

4.2 Execution

Each of the 30 prompts was run once in each engine:

  • ChatGPT (signed in).
  • Gemini (signed in, Automatic mode).
  • Perplexity (signed in).

Critical rule: every prompt ran in a fresh chat, with memory off and no prior context. A single chat for all 30 would have carried prompts 1–29 into prompt 30 and invalidated the measurement.

Total: 90 observations.

4.3 Variables recorded

Per observation: prompt, engine, date and time, full response, brand mention, recommendation, cited URLs, competitors detected.

4.4 Metric definitions

6. The conceptual gap

Six prompts ask for an explanation, not a vendor list:

  1. How is a major overhaul of a centrifugal pump performed?
  2. What services does an oil company need to maintain its rotating equipment?
  3. What criteria should be used when selecting a CNC machining shop?
  4. Which industrial pump types require specialised maintenance in oil operations?
  5. What does preventive, corrective and predictive maintenance include?
  6. What equipment and services are needed to support drilling-rig logistics?

Result: 0 mentions in 18 observations. Zero verifiable citations.

In these answers the engines explain the process in generic technical language and cite no Venezuelan supplier at all. In other words: the space is not held by a competitor — it is empty. Nobody has published the explanation the engines could cite.

The strategic implication is direct. In commercial prompts you compete to appear in a list. In conceptual prompts you compete to be the source of the explanation, and today there is no incumbent.

7.6 Competitive reading

No competitor dominates. The most mentioned in the set is Test Pump (10), followed by TAMEGCA (6) and GESCA (5). IMETAL, with 40 mentions of its own, leads on volume — but its advantage disappears when the prompt is conceptual, where nobody appears. That is the winnable ground.

8. Citations and sources

22 of 90 answers (24.4%) cite imetal.com.ve with a full URL. The rest mention the brand without supporting it with the domain.

Breakdown:

  • ChatGPT: 18 citations out of 30 answers (60%). The most link-generous engine and the easiest to audit.
  • Gemini: 4 verifiable citations, though it labels the domain in more answers through source chips without a full URL.
  • Perplexity: 0 verifiable, due to its interface limitation.

Why it matters: a mention without a citation is reconstructed from memory. It can change between sessions and it generates no traffic. A citation is evidence that the company's content was retrieved and used. For GEO, the citation is the hard metric; the mention is the soft one.

Third-party domains most frequently cited alongside the brand: cosapi.com.ve, testpumpvenezuela.com, mbinter.net, gesca.com.ve, jlmvenezuela.com, sumitorve.com. These are the sources engines rely on to talk about the sector — and therefore the sites where being present matters.

9. Brand representation

What the engines say about IMETAL is mutually consistent:

  • Location: El Tigre, Anzoátegui state. Correct in every case.
  • Age: "founded 1990", "more than 35 years". Consistent with the site.
  • Specialty: centrifugal, reciprocating and screw pump rebuilding; CNC machining; major overhaul.
  • Differentiator they repeat: in-house workshop and machining capability.

No serious factual errors were detected in the descriptions. The weakness is not accuracy, it is coverage: when the brand appears, it is described well.

Identified risk: two engines describe it as a "machinery factory" (Gemini, in C03 and C09, taken from Google Maps). That is an imprecise category that can attract the wrong queries.

11. Strategic recommendations

Immediate (0–30 days).

Publish the six conceptual guides. Each must answer the full question, with steps, criteria and verifiable data (API/ASME standards, tolerances, lead times). These are not service pages; they are technical documents an engineer would cite.

Medium term (30–90 days).

Dedicated content for drilling-rig logistics and compressors. Both lines have zero visibility and are competing against nothing. Reinforce entity markup (structured data, consistent name and category) to correct the "machinery factory" label.

Long term (90–180 days).

Source work: appear on the six domains engines already cite. That is not solved with on-page SEO; it is solved with relationships, sector directories and content others want to link to.

What I do not recommend: optimising for AI "keywords". They do not exist. What exists is content that answers better than the rest and an entity engines recognise without ambiguity.

13. Limitations

  1. Single pass. Generative engines are not deterministic. This study measures one day, not a trend. Repeating with three passes per prompt is the next methodological step.
  2. Three of four relevant surfaces. Google AI Overviews / AI Mode is missing, and it concentrates the largest volume of affected search.
  3. Undisclosed models. ChatGPT, Gemini and Perplexity do not expose the exact model version that answered. Gemini in Automatic mode routes between models depending on the prompt.
  4. Perplexity without URLs. Its interface does not expose source links without manual interaction, so its Citation Rate is not comparable.
  5. Recommendation validated by hand. That is a strength of the study, but also a limit: 40 human judgements, not a reproducible algorithm.
  6. Partially verified competitors. 15 of 172 entities have public evidence. The remaining 156 are long tail, mostly single appearances.

14. Conclusions

  1. IMETAL has visibility, not authority. It appears in 44.4% of answers, but only once the buyer already knows what they want. At the moment criteria are formed, it does not exist.
  2. The brand is well described and badly distributed. No factual errors; absence at the discovery stage.
  3. The winnable ground is empty. Zero mentions in 18 conceptual observations means no competitor has taken that space. It is a window, not a structural problem.
  4. Citations are the metric that matters. The 24.4% verified citation rate is the best predictor of whether the company's content is actually being used.
